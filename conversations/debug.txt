  Here's what was going on.

  The GIL error is not your bug — it's the wreckage

  The timeline is conclusive. Every burst of Attempted to use PyProxy when 
  Python GIL not held / Cannot enter a promising task... immediately follows an
  exceededCpu kill on /api/turn, and the requests that throw it are /api/health,
  /api/prompts, /api/samples — endpoints that burn 0ms CPU and can't possibly
  be at fault. Cloudflare kills the isolate mid-interpreter, Pyodide is left
  wedged, and every subsequent request routed there dies until it recycles. One
  slow turn took out your health check for 17 minutes straight (02:02:16 →
  02:19:50).

  So I fixed the CPU exhaustion, and found two genuine bugs in the streaming
  path along the way.

  Root causes, in order of leverage

  1. Path data volume. Tremor-heavy strokes produce enormous d attributes, which
  get re-emitted by the model, sent back, and sent again next turn. On a
  synthetic 3-second wiggly stroke the new pipeline cuts the path from 8247 → 
  1094 bytes, 86.7%. Three stages in editor.js:20-102: sample spacing 2.5→3.5,
  an EMA tremor filter (SMOOTHING = 0.45), and Ramer–Douglas–Peucker
  simplification. Preview deliberately skips RDP — it re-picks kept points as
  the stroke grows, which reads as the line twitching under the pen.

  2. The pump task was doubly broken (worker/src/entry.py:154-213).
  asyncio.ensure_future(pump()) kept no strong reference — asyncio only weakly
  references tasks, so the pump could be garbage collected mid-stream. That is
  very likely your "thinking text arrives, drawing never shows up": the body
  just stops. It was also detached from the request, which is what produces the
  "promising task" error on the next request to that isolate. Now it's held in
  _PUMPS, handed to waitUntil, and its exceptions are logged.

  3. Frames cost more than the work. One Python→JS crossing per thinking token.
  Now batched to 512 bytes, with status/svg/done/error/progress still going out
  immediately.

  Your three follow-ups

  - Timeouts: the real bug wasn't the number. The answer SVG is buffered (half a
    <path> is a glitch, not a drawing), so the entire drawing phase emitted
    nothing — your 90s stall watchdog was killing turns that were working fine.
    There's now a progress event every 2000 chars, so silence means silence.
    Stall 90s→120s, ceiling 300s→480s, REQUEST_TIMEOUT_S 240→420, ordered so the
    server gives up first and the client gets a real error instead of "gave
    up".
  - Green button, no drawing: takeTurn returned normally when the stream just
    stopped. app.js now tracks whether the turn ever reached svg or error and
    reports a truncated stream as a failure.
